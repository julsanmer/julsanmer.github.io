Initial orbit

Hyperbolic trajectories are accepted: set \(e > 1\) with \(a < 0\) (or define by \(r_p,\ e\)), and \(\lvert\theta_0\rvert < \theta_\infty = \arccos(-1/e)\). They apply to the coplanar-impulse, plane-change and line-of-apsides maneuvers; transfers and phasing need a closed orbit.

Thruster parameters

Tsiolkovsky equation: \(\Delta V = V_e\ln\dfrac{m_0}{m_0-m_F}\), \(V_e = I_{sp}\,g_0\), \(m_F = m_0\bigl(1-e^{-\Delta V/V_e}\bigr)\).

3 · Maneuver

Coplanar velocity triangle at burn point \(\theta_i\)

Phasor solution: \(V_f\angle\varphi = \Delta V\angle\psi + V_i\angle 0^\circ\). Both \(\psi\) and \(\varphi\) are measured from \(\vec V_i\) in the same sense, positive when the flight-path angle increases (\(\Delta\vec V\) tilted towards the outward radial direction); \(\psi = 0^\circ\) is a prograde tangential burn, \(\psi = 180^\circ\) retrograde. The spacecraft coasts from \(\theta_0\) to \(\theta_i\) before the impulse.

General change of coplanar orbit \((a, e, \omega)\)

Orbits must have intersection points between them. There is an intersection if \(A^2+B^2 \ge C^2\) from the equation \(A\sin\theta_i + B\cos\theta_i = C\). This equation can be solved and, given \(\theta_i\), the velocity triangle can be posed.

Change of apoapsis / periapsis and circularization

Rule: to change the apoapsis, burn tangentially at periapsis; to change the periapsis, burn tangentially at apoapsis.

Rotation of the line of apsides

The goal is to rotate the line of apsides by \(\Delta\omega\) keeping the orbit shape \((a, e)\). Accordingly \(V_i = V_f\). This maneuver can be done at two specific points in the orbit.

Hohmann transfer between circular orbits

Hohmann transfer applies two tangential impulses with a transfer ellipse of \(a_H = (r_i + r_f)/2\).

Bi-elliptic transfer

Bi-elliptic transfer applies three tangential impulses (two prograde and one retrograde) with two transfer ellipses of \(a_1 = (r_i + r_t)/2\) and \(a_2 = (r_t + r_f)/2\). The farthest point from the central body \(r_t\) is a design parameter such that \(r_t > r_f\).

Coaxial elliptic ↔ elliptic (or circular) transfer

A Hohmann-based transfer between elliptical orbits with two tangential impulses at periapsis → apoapsis or vice versa. The \(\Delta V\)-efficient transfer passes through the highest apoapsis.

Out-of-plane impulse \((\Omega, i)\)

In the out-of-plane impulse the coplanar parameters \((a, e, \omega, \theta)\) are kept constant so \(V_i = V_f\). The orbital plane orientation will be rotated by \(\Delta i\) and/or \(\Delta\Omega\). By knowing the initial and final orbital inclinations and right ascension of the ascending nodes, spherical trigonometry is used to compute the angle between planes \(\Delta A\). The intersection between planes arises at geographical latitude \(\phi\).

Velocity triangle: \(\lvert\vec V_f\rvert = \lvert\vec V_i\rvert\) with angle \(\Delta A\) between them; \(\Delta V\) is the base of the isosceles triangle.

Three-impulse inclination change (circular orbit)

The goal is to find a more cost efficient maneuver than the inclination change with a single impulse. The key idea is to do the inclination change far from the central body at a radius \(r_t\) where velocity is lower. To do so, a first prograde tangent impulse has to be done to raise the apoapsis. Finally, a third retrograde tangent impulse (antisymmetric to the first one) is done when returning to the initial point to circularize the orbit.

The plane change at \(r_t\) must happen on the line of nodes, so the transfer apoapsis is placed at the ascending node; the first impulse is at the descending node.

Hohmann transfer with inclination change

Combine the radius change and the plane change. The plane change \(\Delta i\) can be applied at the first impulse, the second, or split (fraction \(\alpha\) at the first burn). Each impulse follows the law of cosines: \[\Delta V_1^2 = V_{c1}^2 + V_{t1}^2 - 2V_{c1}V_{t1}\cos(\alpha\,\Delta i),\qquad \Delta V_2^2 = V_{c2}^2 + V_{t2}^2 - 2V_{c2}V_{t2}\cos\bigl((1-\alpha)\Delta i\bigr).\] In a theoretical exercise, we will apply the inclination change at the farthest point. In practice, the optimum split \(\alpha\) is always computed since fuel is scarce in space.

1st burn: \(\vec V_i \to \vec V_H(r_i)\), plane change \(\Delta i_1\).

2nd burn: \(\vec V_H(r_f) \to \vec V_f\), plane change \(\Delta i_2\).

Drawn and animated with the whole plane change at the farther impulse, which is placed on the line of nodes.

Phasing in a circular orbit

Both interceptor and target are in the same circular orbit separated by an angle \(\Delta\nu\). The phasing maneuver inserts the interceptor in a higher (if target is behind) or lower (if target is ahead) altitude orbit of period \(T_{ph} = T\bigl(1 \mp \tfrac{\Delta\nu}{2\pi k}\bigr)\). The integer \(k\) is the number of interceptor revolutions in the phasing orbit.

Phase angle between target and interceptor along the maneuver; the marker follows the animation.

Phasing in an elliptic orbit

When doing phasing in an elliptical orbit, we need to pose the problem with time differences instead of angles. The interceptor is named as B and the target as A. The time difference between them at the interceptor burn time is \(t_{BA} = \Delta t_B(\theta_B) - \Delta t_A(\theta_A)\). Then the interceptor's phasing period has to be \(T_{ph} = T + t_{BA}/k\), with \(k\) the number of revolutions in the phasing orbit. Note that the phasing maneuver will depend on the interceptor's true anomaly \(\theta_B\) at the burn point.
